## Conditions

1. **Foreground, key window.** These are the active-state values that
   round 1 could not measure. The window buttons are coloured, the glass
   shapes have rims, gradients and shadows, and the selection is accent
   blue.
2. **1x on a 100 Hz display.** The recorder writes a frame only when the
   window changes. The gaps between changed frames are therefore the
   app's own cadence, from about 9 ms upward.
3. **Synthetic input with known parameters**, read from the manifests:
   - the linear scrolls are events 8.3 ms apart;
   - the flick is 8 gesture events, then 173 momentum events decaying with
     a 0.33 s time constant;
   - the narrow window is a single resize from 1400 to 1100.

   Durations that follow the input belong to the harness, not to the app.
4. **Two capture artefacts.** The reference's split step failed and left
   its Add Editor menu open. From then on the capture tool drew the
   window together with the menu, scaled to fit. The narrow-window still
   shows the window at 0.898 scale (found from the still's alpha channel:
   988 of 1100 points wide). Its positions are divided by that scale and
   carry about ±2 points. Ycode's Stripes row was not found through
   Accessibility, so its step 05 did nothing.

## Methods

**Colours and geometry** use the same colour runs, glyph ink boxes and
corner fits as round 1. The active toolbar shapes have no flat fill, so
their edges were found as rim pixels: local minima at least 4 levels
darker than the pixels 3 columns to each side.

**Response over the bands.** For every frame of the bands scroll, the
mean colour was taken in fixed regions inside each bar, away from the
bar's own text: the toolbar band above and below the shapes, between two
shapes, the jump bar, and the bottom bar. For each region two frames were
recorded:
- the darkest frame whose colour was neutral (slate content beneath);
- the reddest frame.

The same was done for the first content row, as a reference. Dividing a
bar's darkness by the content's gives the share that shows through. Blur
was judged from a frame amplified four times: the glow follows the band's
outline, its edge spreads over about 15 points, and rows a few points
apart keep different strengths.

**Scroll offsets per frame.** A strip of the document was built from the
before still (offsets 0–756) and the reference's after still (600 lower).
Each frame's rows 400–800 were matched against it with the least mean
absolute difference. This gives the offset per frame for the linear
scroll and the flick, to a point. The flick also covers negative offsets:
the overscroll past the top.

**Pinned header.** For each frame, the header row (y 86–104) was compared
with the header in the after still. A mean difference under 3 levels
means pinned. The first pinned frame was then set against that frame's
offset.

**Springs.** The spring-back after the overscroll was fitted with a
critically damped response, x = A(1 + ωt)e^(−ωt), with ω swept in 0.05
steps. A plain exponential fits five times worse.

**Inspector slide.** On row 856, the leading edge of the inspector is the
first column from the right whose colour leaves the inspector's
background. Line 6's two visual rows show the re-wrap: the right end of
the ink in each row, frame by frame. Line 1's number shows whether the
scroll position moved. The curves were fitted as in round 1, as a grid
over the start time, the end time and the four control values, with
named curves scored on their own.

**Minimap width.** The separator is a column that stays about #E6E6E6
over at least 85 % of the rows. In the scaled still it is a local minimum
column instead. Widths were measured in three editor widths:
- 551 points, in the narrow window;
- 850 points, at rest;
- 1110 points, with the inspector hidden.

**Navigator scroll bar.** Column runs at x 281 in four stills: before the
expansion, after it, and after scrolling. The rule for the knob's length
came from three quantities:
- the 432 points scrolled (both the harness and row matching measured
  it);
- the knob's travel;
- the track's length.

**Timelines.** Pixel changes between frames were counted per region
(navigator, jump bar, editor, inspector, status bar), from the click time
in the manifest. Selection presence is the count of accent-blue pixels in
the navigator, frame by frame.

## Corrections to the judges

- The syntax colours are unchanged in the active window. Gemini's
  #AD3DA4, #D12F1B and teal types do not occur, and Ycode does colour type
  names.
- Both apps draw the linear scroll at about 20 ms per frame. The 13.3
  against 20 ms finding came from mixing the steps.
- The inspector motion is 222 / 216 ms, not 305 / 270 ms. The judges'
  figures include the pressed-state frame and a trailing toolbar frame.
- The 988-point "visible frame" is the scaled still, not a minimum width.
- The reference does show "Loading…" for an image.
- The minimap scale is the same in both apps. Only the bar segmentation
  differs.
- With this Mac's setting the navigator scroll bar is the layout-taking
  style and does not auto-hide.
- The flick's total durations are not comparable. The app-specific part
  is the overscroll and the spring.

## Limits

the harness's manifests. No artwork is described beyond colour families,
and no Apple file was opened except the generated test project.
