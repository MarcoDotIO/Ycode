# OBS-ICON — `.icon`, `actool` and `Assets.car` observations (2026-10-30)

Dirty-room notes behind `specs/formats/icon-format.md`,
`specs/formats/icon-compile.md`, `specs/formats/assets-car.md` and
`specs/formats/README-icon.md`. Prose only; raw output (strings dumps,
`assetutil` JSON, byte dumps, scripts, proof files) is in
`research/dirty/icon/` and is not committed.

## Setup

- Xcode 27.1 (27A9269) as installed on the Mac (not the reference copy);
  Icon Composer 27.1 (build 129) inside it, and the standalone Icon
  Composer 2.0 (build 109.4). `actool` 27.1 (25098), `assetutil` (dump tool
  1011.4).
- No GUI: Icon Composer was never launched. No clicks, no screen use.
- Samples: our own SVGs (a ring, a bar, a dot) and PNGs (a checkerboard, a
  gradient), generated by script. Icon documents written by hand into the
  owner's observation inbox folder (path in local notes). Nothing from
  Apple's templates or sample art was copied anywhere; the Xcode file
  templates (`icon.json` only, no art) and third-party `.icon` files on the
  owner's drive (open-source projects) were read as format samples.
- Load: one `xcodebuild` of a one-file app, about 60 `actool` runs on tiny
  inputs, about 150 `ictool` renders at 256 px; all at `nice 10`.

## Method

1. **Vocabulary.** `strings` on `IconComposerFoundation` gives the Codable
   key names, enum values, error messages and the `ictool` usage text
   (level 4). The third-party files and templates show which keys real
   files use (level 2).
2. **Reader behaviour.** `ictool --export-image` reads a document and
   renders a rendition. Errors (wrong enum values, missing keys) come back
   as one-line messages naming the valid values. Pixel-diffing a render
   against a base document shows whether a key is read and what its default
   is (a variant equal to the base has the default value).
3. **Compile behaviour.** `actool` on the same documents, alone or with an
   `.xcassets`, per platform. Outputs read with `plutil`, `iconutil`, a PNG
   reader, `assetutil --info` and our own container dumper.
4. **Container.** The dumper was written from the public write-ups (Timac
   2018; dbg.re 2026, CC BY-SA 4.0) and checked against the MIT-licensed
   blacktop/ipsw reader. Unknown TLVs (1020, 1021, 1022) and the icon
   records were decoded by changing one `.icon` key at a time and diffing
   the compiled bytes.
5. **Proof writer.** A small test script (dirty room only) wrote cars from
   scratch; `assetutil` and AppKit judged them.

## Findings worth remembering

- `ictool` (in Icon Composer's `Contents/Executables`) is a usable
  black-box renderer: six renditions, three platforms, tint options, design
  generation 26/27. It prints errors on stdout and exits 0.
- Defaults that are not obvious: translucency enabled at **0.3** when the
  key is absent; shadow neutral 0.5; specular on; lighting **individual**;
  layer `glass` on; no `fill` → white in light/tinted and the system dark
  gradient in dark (not the same as `"automatic"`, which is a light
  gradient).
- `dark` specializations do not reach TintedDark/ClearDark; `tinted`
  covers all four tinted and clear renditions. Idiom `square` covers iOS
  and macOS.
- `supported-platforms` is ignored by `actool`. An `.icon` inside the
  catalog is ignored. An `.icon` beats an `.appiconset` of the same name.
  visionOS ignores `.icon` silently; tvOS warns and makes no icon.
- The flattened iOS icon bitmaps in the car are opaque unmasked squares;
  the macOS ones are finished icons (824 pt body at 100,100 plus shadow).
  The loose iOS PNGs are CgBI-crushed when `--compress-pngs` is passed.
- Container gotcha: a BOM tree with a fixed key length must carry copies of
  the keys in the leaf after the entry list. Without them every lookup
  returns the middle entry. Using the "variable length" marker avoids it.
- Attribute numbers in the dbg.re table are shifted by one from 5 on; the
  numbering that matches `assetutil`'s Key Format is in the spec.
- Proof: hand-written cars load in AppKit (images, colours, macOS app icon
  in Finder/`NSWorkspace`), including a car with **only** the layered stack
  (no flattened bitmaps), which the system rendered as a glass icon. The
  iOS-format car parses in `assetutil` exactly like `actool`'s app-icon-set
  output; iOS runtime acceptance was not tested.
